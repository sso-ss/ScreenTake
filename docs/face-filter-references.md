### Dense 3D mesh, partial lips, and fitted lower face
Peach now uses an on-device Core ML conversion of Google's MediaPipe Face Mesh: 468 inferred XYZ vertices and 898 triangles. Resources, Apache 2.0 licensing and provenance are in `Screen/Resources/FaceMesh`. This is relative monocular depth, not measured depth and not a common depth coordinate system with Vision hands. The mesh supplies dense eyes, brows, lips, jaw and nose bridge; triangle normals orient upper lashes without applying the old 2D head-pitch correction a second time. Natural alone retains its existing path; sparse landmarks remain a fallback.

The initial mesh replay detected all 221 nonblack frames, but user screenshots exposed an important validation gap: presence confidence did not establish accurate mouth/chin alignment. Under occlusion, the predicted lower face contracted. The mesh projection now receives a bounded, smooth fit to independently observed current mouth bounds and the stable chin endpoint, retaining its predicted depth and connected topology. Lip pigment uses the current mouth boundary and opening together, avoiding lag and double cutouts from the eye-stabilized geometry. Its startup state no longer delays the first visible mouth after a seek.

Partial lip coverage is handled per pixel after edge feathering, without disabling the entire lip region. Articulated hand masks are combined with source-image protection for dark hair and cool fabric. That protection is relative to the current face's lighting, with regressions at several exposures. Lip pigment also checks excess redness relative to nearby skin, reducing tint on blurred skin-colored fingers while retaining faint overlining. These color/geometry checks are not semantic segmentation and cannot reliably recognize every similarly colored object or heavily blurred finger.

The mesh-fit suite checks actual Core ML depth, connected topology, chin alignment, 3D lash direction, source hair/fabric protection, loss and seek. The separate sparse-path suite covers lashes, masks, partial-lip coverage, foreground shaping and recovery. Recorded comparisons inspect raw/before/after output at the reported mouth, hair and hand poses, including frames 184 and 210. The first corrected replay retained 221/221 nonblack detections, with median 43.8 ms and p95 57.0 ms at 960 px; this does not establish sustained 30 fps. Upward head tilt remains unverified on real footage because the supplied recording covers neutral/downward head pitch.

Final fitted-mask replay: 221/221 nonblack frames have meshes, no whole-lip dropouts, median 43.2 ms / p95 48.4 ms at 960 px. ### Core ML compute-unit comparison
Compared `.cpuAndGPU` with `.all` on the local Apple Silicon Mac using the existing performance-review camera clip and preserved Peach component settings, with makeup enabled for the benchmark. `benchmark_face_mesh.swift` builds with the production detector and filter at `-O`; optional compute-unit injection permits both modes without changing the production default. The benchmark bundle contains the existing compiled model and triangle resource. Hardware/video decoding required execution outside the sandbox; the sandboxed decoder failure is not evidence of damaged footage.

Isolated mesh inference used 22 current-face samples, 12 warmup calls per mode and 176 measured calls per mode, alternating order. Median detection time (including crop, prediction and fitting) was 4.091 ms for `.cpuAndGPU` versus 4.114 ms for `.all`; p95 was 4.607/4.608 ms. Both had zero rejected meshes, and the compared XYZ landmarks and presence scores were identical.

Full-filter replay used the same 204 frames in CPU/GPU, all, all, CPU/GPU order at each resolution, excluding the first 12 frames from timing while checking all frames for detection. Both modes produced 203 meshes per pass; only the initial black frame lacked a face. At 640 px, pooled median was 34.236/34.196 ms and p95 36.745/40.181 ms. At 960 px, median was 43.829/43.625 ms and p95 48.251/49.179 ms. Sampled rendered frames every 30 frames matched exactly. These small median differences do not establish a speedup; one `.all` pass had higher tail latency.

Production remains `.cpuAndGPU`. `.all` permits the Neural Engine but does not force its use; this comparison neither proves which accelerator executed the model nor measures battery consumption, thermal behavior or simultaneous live recording. The isolated mesh's approximately 4 ms cost indicates that the remaining detection, masks and rendering deserve separate profiling. Raw results are saved in `.build/mesh-compute-benchmark/results.json`.

### Detection, masks and rendering profile
`benchmark_face_stages.swift` adds a stage benchmark using the same production sources and camera clip. Compile it with `benchmark_face_mesh.swift`, `-O`, `-D FACE_BEAUTY_PROFILING` and `-D FACE_BEAUTY_STAGE_BENCHMARK`, in a bundle containing the compiled model and triangle resource. The first define enables wall-time checkpoints around the actual Vision batch, separate pose request, mesh, mask preparation, tracking and cosmetic drawing. All checkpoints and diagnostic image capture are excluded from ordinary app builds. Source comparison confirms the ordinary filter is unchanged with the profiling blocks removed; the normal compilation also passes.

Core Image builds lazy graphs, so graph construction alone does not measure GPU processing. The benchmark forces the finished output through `CIContext.createCGImage` and times that separately; this measures rendering, scheduling and output creation rather than GPU kernel time alone. Timed passes do not retain intermediate diagnostic images, since retaining them inflated rendering latency in the initial experiment. Separate graph-render probes include their dependencies and cannot be added/subtracted to reconstruct production stage costs. Standalone face, hand and pose requests also remain diagnostic: their timings cannot be summed as a decomposition of Vision's actual face/hand batch.

Final replay runs use 640, 960, 960, 640 px order, excluding the first 12 frames per pass from timing. All four runs detect 203 faces, 203 meshes and hands in 203 of the same 204 frames; the initial black frame has no face. At 960 px, mean full-filter time is 44.910 ms (median 43.936, p95 51.009). Mean stage costs: face/hand batch 12.694 ms, separate pose 4.937 ms, mesh 4.308 ms, hand/protection/skin mask preparation 1.101 ms, cosmetic drawing/graph construction 3.301 ms, and deferred output rendering 16.929 ms; setup, matching, tracking and cleanup account for the remaining 1.640 ms. Mask GPU work is included in output rendering, so 1.101 ms is not the total cost of masks. Separate 960 px Vision probes have medians of 5.229 ms for faces and 5.709 ms for hands.

At 640 px, full-filter median is 34.797 ms and output-render median 9.496 ms, versus 16.614 ms output rendering at 960 px. The smaller preview is approximately 21% faster in this replay. Detection/pose and deferred rendering are the largest measured targets; replacing the 3D model alone addresses a much smaller share of latency. Results do not measure power, memory pressure, concurrent live capture or dropped recording frames. Raw results are in `.build/face-stage-profile/results.json`.
