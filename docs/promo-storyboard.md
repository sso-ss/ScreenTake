# ScreenTake promo storyboard — draft 1

Story and production plan. The user subsequently authorized a Remotion build; implementation lives in `../promo-remotion/`.

**First-cut asset adaptation:** the initial Remotion film uses native ScreenTake screenshot assets and an illustrated example workflow. It is visibly labeled as a product visualization. The open ScreenTake recording was preserved, and live interaction capture has not been used. The recording-led shots described below remain the footage targets for a future capture pass; the first cut establishes the complete timing, captions, motion, depth and soundtrack.

## Direction

**48 seconds. Music and short captions. Screen footage only.**

The story is one recording becoming a clearer, more polished demo. Open with the benefit, show how ScreenTake creates it, then end by letting the finished result play. Use the existing line **“Your screen. A better take.”** as the opening promise and closing signature.

The intended viewer is a Mac user making product demos, tutorials or walkthroughs. The desired response is: “I can make my screen recording easier to follow, edit it, and finish it in this app.”

## What the project gives us

The current source, release documentation and native screenshots support these feature beats:

- Native display/window capture with 30/60 FPS options and a compact capture toolbar.
- Automatic click zoom, manual zoom, editable zoom sections, strength and focus controls for eligible recordings.
- Arrow, Hand and Circle cursors, adjustable size and recording-time click highlighting.
- Trim, split, remove and reorder sections, with undo/redo and pause review.
- Record or import voiceover, move/trim separate takes, and balance narration against original audio.
- Wallpapers, corners and multiple output ratios, with a live editing preview.
- Apply changes and save the finished video locally.

The existing 48-second website promo already uses floating control crops, perspective, gradients and a presenter overlay. The proposed film keeps the established branding while making the real editing workflow and finished output more prominent. The current editor screenshot is particularly useful for the narration beat: original audio is teal, narration takes are purple, and both appear beneath the video.

For the main film, prioritize zoom, editing, narration and framing. Webcam, phone framing and detailed capture settings can become separate feature clips. The selected screen-only direction gives these core actions time to register.

### Narration beat

Scene 6 demonstrates ScreenTake's voiceover feature visually. Record a real take to obtain the waveform and editable strip, then mute its speech in the trailer soundtrack. Music and captions carry the whole promo, as requested.

### The single demo to carry through the film

Prepare a small, original example interface with two or three meaningful actions: open an item, change a setting, and show the result. Use large enough labels to remain readable in the final output. Avoid filling the source with ScreenTake's marketing copy; the recorded app should have a task, while the trailer captions explain ScreenTake.

Record a short deliberate idle gap between actions. That gap becomes the editing example. Keep the same source content and visual identity through the zoom, cursor, edit, narration, framing and final-playback scenes. A viewer should recognize the original recording in the finished result.

The introductory before/after should be produced from corresponding moments of the same source. Keep timing and content aligned so it demonstrates a presentation improvement rather than a change of task.

## Art direction

**Color.** A charcoal/plum outer stage with ScreenTake violet **#6C5CE7** as the main accent. Use lilac light very subtly around the stage. Let the product's actual wallpapers provide the larger color moments: Midnight for the opening atmosphere, Prism for a brief transformation, Lagoon for the finished result. Avoid cycling every wallpaper. Teal and purple audio colors retain their existing meaning.

**Tone.** Calm confidence with moments of speed. The film starts quickly, spends time on useful behavior, then slows down for the finished demo and product name. Every claim has visible support.

**Depth.** A consistent three-layer space: soft backdrop, product surface, occasional detail crop. Give floating surfaces a restrained shadow and edge light. Use perspective around transitions and overview shots; keep interactive proof close to front-facing. Important labels, timeline handles and output content remain sharp. Blur can separate background from foreground, but should not obscure the action.

**Typography.** Use one clean sans-serif family compatible with the native Mac look. Sentence-case captions, one or two lines, usually three to six words. Highlight only a key word or phrase with violet when helpful. Put captions in consistent clear space instead of moving them to a different corner on every shot.

**Movement.** Use fast reveals followed by readable holds. Suggested trailer timing ranges: 0.3–0.5 seconds for a reframing reveal, 0.25–0.4 seconds for an isolated detail crop, 1.2–2 seconds for a caption to register. These are creative starting points. Product zoom timing comes from the actual output. Avoid adding a second zoom over the product zoom or giving every surface a continuous wobble.

## Remotion production and depth treatment

**Production framework: Remotion.** The nine storyboard scenes use timed React compositions/sequences with frame-driven movement, caption layers and a coordinated soundtrack. The first cut uses native screenshot assets and reconstructed example interactions, with the recording-led footage targets retained below.

The user's “DOP depth” is interpreted here as **depth of field (DOF), layered spatial depth and perspective**.

Use a consistent layer stack:

1. **Backdrop:** softly defocused plum/charcoal atmosphere, with subtle light movement.
2. **Product surface:** actual ScreenTake footage, sharp at the current point of attention, with restrained perspective in overview and transition shots.
3. **Detail crop:** occasional enlarged footage of a control or timeline action, separated by overlap, shadow and a small difference in motion.
4. **Captions:** sharp, readable text positioned above the scene.

The default construction uses **2.5D perspective and simulated DOF**: perspective transforms, independent layer movement, restrained shadows and animated blur where it helps establish focus. This is a compositing treatment, not automatically a physically accurate lens simulation. A true 3D plane/camera setup can be evaluated for a specific shot if the desired selective focus cannot be achieved convincingly with compositing.

Depth varies by scene:

- **Opening:** strong scale contrast, soft background and a rapid reveal of the sharp finished output.
- **Recording, zoom and cursor proof:** minimal lens effects; readable, nearly front-facing footage. The product's own zoom remains visually distinct from trailer camera movement.
- **Editor introduction:** gentle tilt and parallax, then settle toward a readable timeline. A subtle focus shift may lead from the overview toward the timeline without blurring the action.
- **Narration:** foreground controls and waveform stay sharp; surrounding context can soften slightly.
- **Framing showcase:** spatial separation and controlled shadow; preserve source proportions and readable preview states.
- **Finished result:** sharp, generous playback with a quiet backdrop.
- **End card:** an optional brief focus reveal resolves fully before the final CTA hold.

Use deterministic frame-based animation so the rendered timing is repeatable. Review the depth treatment at delivery resolution: small text, pointer edges and timeline handles must remain clear. Do not apply uniform blur across the entire UI to imitate Linear's selective focus.

## Music and sound plan

Use a restrained instrumental electronic track, approximately 100–110 BPM, with a clear pulse and room for clicks. Choose a properly licensed track during production. The planned scene boundaries can be adjusted slightly to its actual structure.

- Opening: a soft rise and clear accent on the raw-to-polished switch.
- Capture and cursor: restrained click sounds tied to visible actions.
- Timeline: a small percussive accent when the unwanted section disappears.
- Narration: keep the pulse quiet while the new audio strip appears; omit speech from the trailer mix.
- Framing: two quiet accents for ratio changes.
- Ending: simplify the music and let the final chord resolve under the held CTA.

The current website promo contains no audio stream, so soundtrack and sound design would be a new production layer. Musical synchronization has not been specified from the four references' audio; the reference study was visual.

## Accuracy notes for production

- Use a ScreenTake-origin recording for cursor and zoom editing; ordinary imported finished footage does not regain captured mouse data.
- Current smart zoom remains experimental. Review the specific captured result before using it as the hero shot.
- Show click highlighting from a recording made with it enabled. Do not fabricate a post-recording highlighting toggle absent from the current editor.
- Show actual split/removal and narration behavior. The film can shorten elapsed time with cuts, while preserving the action/result relationship.
- The visible save workflow uses Apply Changes, then Download to save locally. Do not invent a cloud publishing action or broad export-format menu.
- Ratio changes preserve the recording's proportions. Avoid stretching the source to fill each new shape.
- The product and source review supports this storyboard; this planning pass has not tested live capture or export hardware behavior.
